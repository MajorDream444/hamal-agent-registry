## 1. The core idea

AMA Solutions is the client-facing agentic agency. Its organizing vision is a campus of **144 specialized Art Mob AGInts, arranged in 12 Mobs of 12**. People should enter through their real problem, discover the relevant Mob and agents, see how those agents work together, and start a conversation about a scoped engagement. The campus is both an understandable map of capabilities and a story world that can grow into named characters, spaces, episodes, and hireable teams.

**HAMAL** names the operating alliance: **H** for Hanzo, **AMA** for AMA Solutions and its Art Mob AGInts, and **L** for Lux. Hanzo supplies the AI and technical capability; AMA owns client discovery, solution design, delivery and commercial relationship; Lux is the proposed provenance and trust layer where an engagement genuinely needs it. **HAMAL is the system and playbook framework that coordinates the work; it is not a thirteenth Mob.** Do not claim all three layers are deployed on every project. AMA Solutions Corp is the lead contracting entity when a contract is in scope. The campus is a digital narrative and interface, not an assertion that a physical campus currently exists.

A simple public explanation: “Tell us what you are building. We assemble the right specialist agents and human leads, map the work into a clear playbook, and deliver work you can review.”

## 6. HAMAL playbook from first contact to handoff

1. **Enter.** Visitor chooses a problem such as “grow revenue,” “build a product,” “tell our story,” “make operations work,” “protect trust,” or “develop leaders.” Offer a free-text route for unusual requests.
2. **Understand.** Ask name, role, organization, goal, current obstacle, desired output, time frame, available systems, data sensitivity and preferred contact method. Ask only what is needed; explain how information will be used.
3. **Diagnose.** A routing agent or human maps the request to one lead Mob and optional collaborators. The older readiness CSV supplies nine sample questions across foundation, opportunity and ecosystem, but its scoring weights are illustrative and incomplete; do not present its total as a validated assessment.
4. **Check scope.** Identify permissions, source data, dependency on Hanzo or Lux, decision owner, budget range and required human review. If fit is uncertain, route to a human discovery call.
5. **Propose.** Present a short project brief: problem, success measure, lead Mob, specialist roles, playbook stages, concrete deliverables, timeline assumptions, client responsibilities, approval gates, fee and optional ongoing support. No automatic commitment from an intake conversation.
6. **Authorize and prepare.** AMA confirms contract and consent; assigns accountable human; grants least-privilege tool access; sets acceptance criteria and a rollback or escalation route for consequential changes.
7. **Execute.** Agents research, create, analyze or coordinate within approved scope; human leads approve consequential decisions and external actions. Record sources, work versions, exceptions and feedback where applicable.
8. **Review and deliver.** Check factual accuracy, quality, privacy, brand fit and agreed criteria. Deliver artifacts, a clear explanation of what was done, and any client training or change request path.
9. **Learn.** Collect client feedback and outcome measures. Update the playbook and agent status after review, never silently change promises mid-engagement.

**Illustrative path:** A founder asks for a launch. Strategists owns positioning; Visionaries tests the longer thesis; Dreamweavers develops the story; Provocateurs prepares the campaign; Artisans defines measurement; Maestros connects intake and reporting; Vanguards reviews sensitive data. AMA’s delivery lead signs off on proposal, public claims and launch. This is a suggested configuration, not an assertion that this pod is currently operational.

## 7. The website experience

### First screen

Headline direction: **“Meet the agents behind your next move.”** Supporting copy: “AMA Solutions brings specialist AI agents and human judgment into one coordinated team. Explore the Mobs, tell us what you are building, and we will map a practical path.” Primary CTA: **“Find my team.”** Secondary CTA: **“Explore the campus.”** Offer a direct **“Talk to AMA”** action for buyers who do not want to browse a fictional world.

A cinematic miniature campus works as an invitation: a central commons with twelve legible district entrances, subtle life and movement, and a visible path from the front gate to the relevant Mob. Show real service labels beside names. Keep loading light, navigation keyboard accessible, and a full text directory for low bandwidth and reduced motion. The campus is not the only way to reach contact.

### Suggested information architecture

| Route | Purpose | Principal action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/` | Promise, proof, campus preview, ways to engage | Find my team |
| `/campus` | Visual district map and searchable Mob directory | Explore a Mob |
| `/mobs` | Twelve Mob cards with problems and specialties | View Mob |
| `/mobs/[slug]` | Mission, lead when verified, specialists, workflows, samples | Discuss a project |
| `/agents/[slug]` | Only verified named profiles; status and supervision clear | Ask about this agent |

| `/about` | AMA, founder, human leadership, Hanzo and proposed Lux role | Meet the team |
| `/start` | Guided needs brief and human discovery option | Submit brief |

The visual journey is **front gate → pick a need → matching district → Mob → agent or pod → guided brief → human follow-up**. Every Mob card should also support a direct inquiry. On mobile, favor a searchable list with a small visual map; do not force people to navigate twelve tiny buildings. Never require registration merely to learn what a Mob does.

### CTA and intake behavior

- **Find my team:** short guided intake; show a tentative match with reasons and alternatives; ask permission before submission. Phrase the result as a recommendation, not an automated diagnosis.
- **Explore the campus:** immersive mode with accessible directory toggle, deep links for every district and an explicit concept label if visuals depict future facilities.
- **Talk to AMA:** contact or scheduling route backed by a working mailbox or booking endpoint before launch.
- **Discuss a project / Ask about this agent:** prefill the Mob or verified agent reference while allowing visitors to change their goal.
- **Build a pod:** optional later feature after roster, availability, scoping and quote workflow are real. Avoid instant checkout for undefined custom work.

A successful CTA produces a confirmation, reference or saved inquiry, clear expected response process, internal owner, and secure handling. If intake is not connected, use a working contact route; never simulate a successful submission.

## 9. Design language and storytelling

The campus should feel like a place built for purposeful work: twelve distinctive spaces arranged around a shared coordination hub. Give every district a recognizable silhouette, color accent, action verb and service statement; use characters and motion to show handoffs between Mobs. A short introductory sequence can move from the gate through three sample requests, then let visitors choose their own route. Agent portraits, backstories and lore can enrich the experience once tied to stable IDs and marked as narrative where fictional.

The emotional sequence is **curiosity → recognition of my problem → trust in the process → clarity on what I can buy → easy human contact**. A cinematic teaser should never conceal basic business facts. Avoid implying sentience, physical staff, a continuously active 144-agent workforce, guaranteed autonomy or verified blockchain provenance solely because the visual story suggests it.
